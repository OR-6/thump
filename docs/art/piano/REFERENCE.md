# Piano keyboard: reference and measurements

A real keyboard: white keys 23.5 mm apart (22.5 mm key, 1 mm gap), 150 mm long; black keys 11 mm wide, 95 mm long, 10 mm proud, bevelled; two black keys over C D E, three over F G A B, set off-centre the way pianos do (C# and F# a little left, D# and A# a little right, G# centred); white keys notched round them; a case with end cheeks, a felt strip behind the keys, a lip in front.

Thump uses exactly these key sizes for two octaves (14 white keys, 10 black, 33 cm of keys, 41 cm with the case and the chord button), so it fits a tray. Every white key plays a note of the chosen scale (so no note is wrong) with the song's tune on the middle keys; a black key plays its two neighbours together. A red rubber button on the case's left plays a chord: a real arranger keyboard has buttons like it, and it replaces the old taped chord key.
